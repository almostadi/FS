## Examples, Illustrations & Test-Your-Knowledge — Topic-wise Summary

## PART A — EXAMPLES (Conceptual/Comment-type)

### Example 1 — Disclosure of source of bonus shares
**Topic:** Schedule III disclosure requirements — bonus shares
**Question (gist):** Alpha Ltd. disclosed in notes that 24,000 bonus shares were issued during the year but did not disclose the *source* from which they were issued. Is this a Schedule III violation?
**Key takeaway:** Schedule III requires disclosure of the aggregate number/class of bonus shares allotted in the 5 years preceding the balance sheet date — but it does **not** require disclosure of the source of such bonus shares. Hence, non-disclosure of source is **not** a violation.

### Example 2 — Disclosure of Work-in-Process (WIP)
**Topic:** Schedule III disclosure requirements — inventory/WIP
**Question (gist):** Loyal Ltd. did not separately disclose opening and closing WIP, arguing the two are roughly equal.
**Key takeaway:** Schedule III does not require opening/closing WIP amounts to be shown in the Statement of P&L — only the **change** in WIP needs disclosure. Non-disclosure is acceptable if the difference between opening and closing WIP is **not material**.

### Example 3 — Negative "Reserves and Surplus" presentation
**Topic:** Presentation of debit balance of P&L within Reserves & Surplus
**Question (gist):** Futura Ltd. had Securities Premium ₹80L, Capital Reserve ₹60L, General Reserve ₹90L (total ₹230L) and an accumulated loss of ₹250L, which it showed as an **asset** under "Statement of Profit and Loss."
**Key takeaway:** A debit balance of P&L (after all appropriations) must be shown as a **negative figure under "Surplus."** If it exceeds total reserves, the entire "Reserves and Surplus" head is shown as a **negative figure on the face of the Balance Sheet** — never as an asset. Futura Ltd.'s treatment (negative ₹20L) was **incorrect**.

### Example 4 — Classification of a bank loan repayable in instalments
**Topic:** Current vs. non-current classification of borrowings
**Question (gist):** Sumedha Ltd. took a ₹10,00,000 loan, repayable in 10 half-yearly instalments of ₹1,00,000, first due 30.09.20X1. Classify as at 31.3.20X1.
**Key takeaway:** Instalments due within the next 12 months (here, 30.09.20X1 & 31.03.20X2 = ₹2,00,000) are shown as **"current maturities of long-term debt"** under **Short-Term Borrowings**; the remaining ₹8,00,000 stays under **Long-Term Borrowings**.

### Example 5 — Provisions vs. Reserves
**Topic:** Distinguishing "Reserves" from "Provisions"
**Question (gist):** Prince Ltd. presented its "provisions for contingencies" under "Reserves and Surplus." Correct?
**Key takeaway:** A **Reserve** is an appropriation of profit for a general/specific purpose (not for a known liability); a **Provision** (per AS-29) is a present obligation that can be measured only by estimation. Provisions must **not** be clubbed under Reserves and Surplus — Prince Ltd.'s treatment is **incorrect**.

### Example 6 — Trade receivables disclosure (Division I)
**Topic:** Schedule III disclosures for Trade Receivables
**Question (gist):** What disclosures apply to Anek Ltd.'s trade receivables under Division I?
**Key takeaway:** Trade receivables must be split into **long-term/short-term**, then sub-classified as **Secured-good / Unsecured-good / Doubtful**; allowance for bad & doubtful debts shown under relevant heads; debts due from directors/officers/related firms shown **separately**; and an **ageing schedule** (buckets: <6 months, 6m–1yr, 1–2yr, 2–3yr, >3yr) must be given for Undisputed-good, Undisputed-doubtful, Disputed-good, and Disputed-doubtful categories.

## PART B — ILLUSTRATIONS (Numerical Problems)

### Illustration 1 — Dividend out of Reserves
**Topic:** Companies (Declaration of Dividend out of Reserves) Rules, 2014
**Question (gist):** XYZ Ltd. wants to declare a 10% dividend despite inadequate current profit, drawing the shortfall from General Reserves. Given preference/equity capital, reserves, current profit and 3-year average dividend rate of 12%, determine the amount that can be drawn from reserves.
**Key takeaway:** Three conditions must all be satisfied — (i) proposed rate ≤ 3-year average rate; (ii) amount drawn ≤ 10% of (paid-up capital + free reserves); (iii) post-drawal reserve balance ≥ 15% of paid-up capital. Worked answer: **₹6,57,500** can be drawn from reserves (all three conditions satisfied).

### Illustration 2 — Full financial statements from Trial Balance (with revaluation & asset sale)
**Topic:** Balance Sheet + Statement of P&L preparation as per Schedule III
**Question (gist):** Omega Ltd.'s trial balance includes land to be revalued, a suspense account representing sale proceeds of part of the machinery, and a final dividend declared **after** the year-end.
**Key takeaway:** Shows the mechanics of a **Revaluation Reserve** (revalued amount − cost), computing **profit on sale of machinery** via a suspense account, deriving depreciation using cost less disposals, and confirms that a dividend declared **after the balance sheet date is not recognised as a liability** — it is disclosed in notes only.

### Illustration 3 — Regrouping a detailed Trial Balance into Schedule III format
**Topic:** Balance Sheet + Statement of P&L preparation — regrouping numerous ledger heads
**Question (gist):** Haria Chemicals Ltd.'s trial balance has ~25 line items (goodwill, preliminary expenses, loose statistical/expense heads) to be regrouped.
**Key takeaway:** Demonstrates how varied ledger balances (discount, commission, rentals, dealer aids, transit insurance, distribution freight, etc.) are consolidated under Schedule III's standard heads — **Cost of materials consumed** (opening + purchases − closing inventory), **Change in inventory of finished goods**, **Employee benefit expense**, **Finance costs**, and a single **Other expenses** note.

### Illustration 4 — Hotel company with share forfeiture & capital reserve
**Topic:** Revenue classification (products vs. services) + share capital adjustments
**Question (gist):** International Hotels Ltd. — split revenue into wines/food (products) and rooms/billiards (services); account for a shareholder's forfeited shares reissued at a premium; write off preliminary expenses and goodwill considerations; depreciate land/building and furniture.
**Key takeaway:** Revenue from Operations must be split into **"Sale of products"** and **"Sale of services."** A forfeited-and-reissued share transaction generates a **Capital Reserve** [No. of shares × (reissue price − forfeited/called-up amount)]. Final dividend declared post year-end again disclosed in notes only.

### Illustration 5 — Balance Sheet only, with loan/bank/receivable disclosures
**Topic:** Balance Sheet preparation — loan splitting, scheduled vs. non-scheduled bank, ageing
**Question (gist):** Pioneer Ltd.'s trial balance gives cost and accumulated depreciation of PPE separately; a State Finance Corporation loan balance includes interest accrued but not due; part of bank balance is with a non-scheduled bank; some receivables are overdue >6 months; a capital contract for machinery is incomplete.
**Key takeaway:** **Interest accrued but not due** on a secured loan is carved out and shown under **Other Current Liabilities** (not clubbed with the loan principal). Bank balances are split into **Scheduled vs. Other (non-scheduled) banks**. Incomplete capital contracts are disclosed as a **note** (capital commitment), not a liability.

### Illustration 6 — Balance Sheet + P&L with tax provision
**Topic:** Full financial statements with income-tax provision, asset sale, revaluation
**Question (gist):** Delta Ltd.'s trial balance uses "Adjusted Purchases" (i.e., purchases already net of inventory movement), a suspense account for machinery sold, and requires a 30% tax provision.
**Key takeaway:** When "Adjusted Purchases" is given, **do not** separately show "Change in inventories" for that item since it's already netted — only closing inventory appears on the Balance Sheet. Profit before tax is computed first, then **tax expense = 30% of PBT**, giving Profit for the period.

## PART C — TEST YOUR KNOWLEDGE

### Q1 (MCQ) — Trade payables composition
**Topic:** Definition of Trade Payables under Schedule III
**Question (gist):** Which item is part of "Trade payables"? Options: statutory dues / interest accrued on trade payables / bills payable / bills receivable.
**Answer & takeaway:** **(c) Bills payable.** Trade payables cover dues for goods/services in the ordinary course of business (including bills payable); statutory dues, interest accrued thereon, and bills receivable are excluded/classified elsewhere.

### Q2 (MCQ) — Securities Premium Account placement
**Topic:** Balance sheet classification of Securities Premium
**Question (gist):** Under which head is Securities Premium shown?
**Answer & takeaway:** **(a) Reserves and Surplus.** It is not part of Share Capital.

### Q3 (MCQ) — "Fixed assets held for sale"
**Topic:** Current vs non-current asset classification
**Question (gist):** How are fixed assets held for sale classified?
**Answer & takeaway:** **(a) Current asset** — since realisation is expected within the operating cycle/short term.

### Q4 (MCQ) — Current maturities of long-term debt
**Topic:** Reclassification of long-term borrowings
**Question (gist):** Where do current maturities of long-term debt appear?
**Answer & takeaway:** **(b) Short-term borrowings** — the portion due within 12 months is moved out of long-term borrowings.

### Q5 (MCQ) — Pre-conditions for dividend declaration
**Topic:** Sec 123(2) — depreciation before dividend
**Question (gist):** Dividend for the current year is declared after providing for what depreciation?
**Answer & takeaway:** **(b) Depreciation on current-year assets AND any arrears of depreciation from past years.** Dividend cannot be declared by skipping unprovided past depreciation.

### Q6 (Theory) — Classification of six specific items under Schedule III
**Topic:** Schedule III head-mapping for tricky quasi-equity/liability items
**Question (gist):** Classify: (i) share application money received in excess of issued capital, (ii) share option outstanding account, (iii) unpaid matured debentures + interest accrued, (iv) uncalled liability on partly-paid shares/investments, (v) calls unpaid, (vi) money received against share warrants.
**Key takeaway (answers):**
- (i) Current Liabilities / Other Current Liabilities
- (ii) Shareholders' Funds / Reserves & Surplus
- (iii) Current Liabilities / Other Current Liabilities
- (iv) Contingent Liabilities and Commitments
- (v) Shareholders' Funds / Share Capital (deducted)
- (vi) Shareholders' Funds / Money received against share warrants

### Q7 (Scenario) — Bose and Sen Ltd. Balance Sheet
**Topic:** Full Balance Sheet preparation integrating multiple disclosure rules
**Question (gist):** Prepare the Balance Sheet as on 31.3.20X1 from ledger balances involving shares allotted for land/building consideration, unsecured loans (bank/related parties/others), a State Finance Corporation loan (partly current), receivables overdue >6 months, a non-scheduled bank deposit, and preliminary expenses to be written off against General Reserve.
**Key takeaway:** A comprehensive integration exercise — shares-for-non-cash-consideration disclosure, splitting a secured term loan into current/non-current portions, unsecured loan breakup by counterparty, receivables ageing, and writing off preliminary expenses directly against opening Reserves (per instructions) rather than through P&L.

### Q8 (Scenario) — Alpha Ltd. Balance Sheet
**Topic:** Balance Sheet with proposed dividend & reserve transfer
**Question (gist):** Similar full Balance Sheet preparation; also involves a proposed transfer to General Reserve and a Board-declared 5% dividend on 2nd April (i.e., after year-end).
**Key takeaway:** Confirms twice-tested principle: **dividends declared after the balance sheet date are disclosed in notes only**, not recognised as a liability; a **proposed transfer to reserve** is shown as an appropriation added to General Reserve out of current year's profit.

### Q9 (Scenario) — Ring Ltd. — full P&L + Balance Sheet
**Topic:** Complete Statement of Profit & Loss and Balance Sheet with tax and provision adjustments
**Question (gist):** Prepare P&L and Balance Sheet given closing stock movement, a fresh doubtful-debts provision (4% of debtors) to be adjusted against the existing provision, 30% tax provision, depreciation split between building/machinery, a post-year-end dividend + reserve transfer, and bills discounted but not yet matured.
**Key takeaway:** Full worked example of the Schedule III **Statement of Profit and Loss** format (Revenue from operations, Other income, Cost of materials, Change in inventories, Employee benefits, Finance costs, Other expenses, Depreciation, Tax expense) paired with the Balance Sheet; **bills discounted but not matured** are disclosed as a **Contingent Liability** in notes, not as a balance sheet liability.

### Q10 (Scenario) — SR Ltd. Balance Sheet
**Topic:** Balance Sheet with multi-source loan splitting and asset classification nuance
**Question (gist):** Prepare the Balance Sheet given a Public Finance Corporation loan (with interest accrued but not due), other long-term unsecured loans from a bank and from directors (partly current), short-term borrowings from a bank and directors, unpaid dividend, receivables ageing, a non-scheduled bank balance, and loose tools included in finished-goods inventory.
**Key takeaway:** Reinforces splitting **each** long-term loan (bank loan, director loan, SFC/PFC loan) between its **current maturity** (short-term borrowings) and **remaining balance** (long-term borrowings); and confirms that **loose tools not meeting the PPE definition under AS 10** are classified as part of **Inventories**, not Property, Plant & Equipment.
